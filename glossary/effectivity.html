Effectivity

Also called: Date effectivity, serial effectivity, cut-in

The rule that says from when, or from which unit, a change applies, so old and new versions can coexist without breaking orders in flight.

RelatedEngineering change (ECR, ECO, ECN)150% BOMSKU rationalization

Effectivity answers the most practical question in engineering change: which orders get the new version? Without it, every change would apply to everything at once, including the 300 orders already being built.

Kinds of effectivity

TypeApplies fromCommon use
DateAn order or build dateConfigure-to-order products, price and option changes
SerialA unit serial numberMachinery, vehicles, anything serviced by serial
LotA production batchProcess and material changes

Choosing the date

The right cut-in date is a negotiation between engineering (as soon as possible), purchasing (when the old stock runs out), the plant (not in the middle of a big run) and sales (not the week before a major project ships). Picking it well is half the change.

Effectivity in a configurator

In a configurator, effectivity usually lives on BOM lines and rules. The same configuration ordered on 30 November and on 1 December can legitimately produce different BOMs. The Rules Lab shows this: the return lever switches from rev A to rev B on 1 December 2026, and a finish becomes unorderable on 1 January 2027, without anyone editing an order.

Questions people ask

What happens to orders placed before the cut-in?

They keep the old version, unless the change is urgent enough (a safety issue, for example) to justify reworking them.

Can effectivity be used to retire options?

Yes. An end date on an option is the cleanest way to phase it out while protecting open quotes.
